How the numbers work

  1. Fair expectation. Each group is compared with everyone else in the dataset born in the same decade and country. Slow planets like Pluto mark birth years and birth seasons vary by country, so a plain "1 in 12" would mislead.
  2. Effect size first. The ratio shows how many more or fewer people have the factor than expected, with a 95% range of plausible values.
  3. Many tests, corrected. Test enough ideas and some will look significant by luck. Every result is adjusted (Benjamini-Hochberg) for all the tests you ran this session.
  4. Confirm on fresh data. 30% of people are held back. A finding is only convincing if it shows up again in data you did not explore.
  5. Small groups hidden. Counts under 10 are not shown.
  6. Who is in the data matters. Famous people are not a random sample of humanity. A Sun-sign result can come from school cut-off dates (older children excel in youth sports), not the sky.

Without a birth time the Ascendant and houses are unknown, and fast factors like the Moon's sign are used only when they were the same all day.
