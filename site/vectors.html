Chart as a vector

How a whole natal chart becomes a list of numbers that a computer can compare and learn from.

Where this fits

Testing one factor at a time ("Mars in Aries") misses combinations. To look at whole charts, every chart first has to become the same-length list of numbers, called a vector. This page is step 1. The boosted-trees and graph neural network pages are two different models that learn from these numbers.

Charts below are computed live in your browser with Astronomy Engine, using the same tropical, geocentric positions as the rest of the site, at 12:00 UTC.

Step 1: A planet's place is a point on a circle

A planet's position is an angle, its ecliptic longitude λ, from 0° (0° Aries) to 360°. There are three common ways to store it, and only one of them keeps "close in the sky" equal to "close in the numbers."

Sign only (one-hot): 12 slots, a 1 in the planet's sign and 0 elsewhere Raw degrees: λ On the circle: ( cos λ , sin λ ) Distance on the circle between λA and λB (the straight-line chord): d = √[(cos λA − cos λB)² + (sin λA − sin λB)²] = 2 · sin(Δ / 2) where Δ is the shorter angle between them (0° to 180°)

Move the two points. Try 29° Aries against 1° Taurus: one-hot calls them completely different signs, raw degrees calls them close, and the circle agrees. Then try 359° against 1°: raw degrees says they're as far apart as possible, but they're 2° apart in the sky.

Step 2: A whole chart is 20 numbers

Do that for all 10 planets and a chart becomes 20 numbers: the cosine and sine of each planet's longitude. Every sign combination is in there automatically, because two charts only look alike when most of their planets are close.

v(chart) = [ cos λ☉, sin λ☉, cos λ☽, sin λ☽, … , cos λ♇, sin λ♇ ] (20 numbers, each from −1 to 1)

The chart's vector. Each column is one planet. The top row is cos λ and the bottom row is sin λ. Blue cells are positive and orange cells are negative; the stronger the color, the further from 0.

Step 3: How similar are two charts?

The distance between two charts adds up the planet-by-planet chord distances from step 1. A weight w lets you count some planets more than others; this page uses equal weights.

D(A, B) = √[ Σ over planets w · dₚ² ] dₚ = 2 · sin(Δₚ / 2) Similarity = 1 − D / Dmax (1 = identical charts, 0 = every planet opposite)

Bars show each planet's chord distance, from 0 (same place) to 2 (opposite sides). Hover a bar for the exact positions.

Step 4: Why "similar charts" mostly means "similar birth dates"

This chart compares Chart A from step 3 with every chart from 15 years before to 15 years after. The fast planets (Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars) come back to nearly the same place every year, so their line dips sharply on every birthday. The slow planets (Jupiter to Pluto) are only close for people born within about a year of each other, with a partial dip about 12 years away, when Jupiter comes back around.

Fast planets: Sun, Mercury, Venus, MarsSlow planets: Jupiter to Pluto

That's the main trap. The people whose charts are most like yours are mostly people born near your birthday, in years close to yours. They share a generation and a season, and those alone shape careers and health. So any whole-chart test has to beat a birth-date-only baseline: a model that knows the birth year and month but nothing else about the sky. Only the improvement over that baseline counts as an astrology signal.

The Moon is left out of both lines. It circles the zodiac every 27 days, so at this scale it looks random. That also makes it one of the few factors that season and generation can't explain.

Step 5: Aspects as strengths, not on/off switches

The test page counts an aspect as either there or not. A model learns more from how tight the aspect is. For each pair of planets, each major aspect gets a strength that is 1 when exact and falls to 0 at the edge of its orb.

separation θ = shorter angle between the two planets (0° to 180°) strength = max(0, 1 − |θ − aspect angle| / orb) conjunction 0° orb 8 · sextile 60° orb 4 · square 90° orb 7 · trine 120° orb 7 · opposition 180° orb 8
